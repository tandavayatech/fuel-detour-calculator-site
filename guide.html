How to use it

Everything the app can do, tab by tab — and the two checkboxes that keep your MPG figures honest.

00

Before you start

The Calculator and Prices tabs work completely stand-alone — no setup needed. Adding a vehicle is only for the Fuel Log and Stats tabs, which track your efficiency over time.

To use the Calculator straight away: open the Calculator tab, enter the detour distance and both prices, then type your vehicle's efficiency by hand. To use Prices, just search — no vehicle needed there either.

If you'd like your efficiency worked out and tracked automatically instead of typing it in each time, add a vehicle:

  1. Open the Settings tab.
  2. Tap Add Vehicle.
  3. Give it a name you'll recognise — "Focus," "Work Van," or a registration plate all work. Each vehicle needs its own unique name.

Record your fill-ups on the Fuel Log tab and your results appear on Stats; the Calculator can then use your vehicle's real average efficiency instead of a typed figure. Everything vehicle-related — the calculator's average, your stats, your log — is scoped to whichever vehicle is currently selected. Choose one from the dropdown at the top of the Calculator, Fuel Log or Stats tab (they all share the same selection), or tap it in the vehicle list on the Settings tab. The Calculator and Stats dropdowns also have a Clear selection option.

If you open the Fuel Log or Stats tab without a vehicle selected, the message on screen has a tappable Settings link that takes you straight there. The first time you open the app, a short welcome tour also covers this — and you can replay it any time with the Show welcome tour button on the Settings tab.

Look out for the (i) icons throughout the app — tap one for a plain-English explanation of whatever it sits next to.

01

Calculator

Works out the minimum amount of fuel you'd need to buy at a cheaper detour to make the trip worth it.

The calculator is split into four cards — Vehicle, Detour distance, Efficiency and Prices — each with an (i) icon explaining it. Whatever you enter is remembered, even after you close the app, so you can pick up where you left off.

Vehicle (optional)

Pick a vehicle to use its fuel log for your average efficiency and tank-size estimate. You can also leave it clear and simply type your own efficiency figure — the calculator works either way.

Detour distance

Two modes: Manual, where you type the extra distance the detour adds directly, or Calculate, where you enter the length of your normal route and the length of the detour route — the app works out the difference for you.

Efficiency

The efficiency unit — MPG (UK), MPG (US), or L/100km — is chosen on the Settings tab. Type a figure by hand, or tick Use average once you've logged two or more fill-ups, to use your vehicle's real calculated efficiency instead of a guess.

Prices, then calculate

Enter the current price (your usual stop or normal route) and the detour price in £ per litre. The Calculate button stays pinned at the bottom of the screen — tap it (or press Done on the keyboard) and the answer appears in a popup. Tap Close, tap outside it, or press Back to dismiss it.

Read the result

Litres to buy

The minimum litres you'd need to buy at the detour station for its cheaper price to outweigh the extra fuel burned getting there, rounded up. The detour price has to be lower than your current price for the detour to ever pay off.

Percentage of a fill-up

With a vehicle that has at least one fill-up logged, that figure is shown as a percentage of your largest fill-up so far — our best guess at your tank size. The gauge turns red above 100%, meaning it may not fit in your tank.

Save or pay extra

Compares buying a largest-fill-up's worth of fuel at the detour station against not detouring and paying your current price — how much you'd save, or how much more it would cost.

We don't know how much fuel is already in your tank, so always compare the result with your actual fuel gauge.

02

Fuel Log

Every fill-up you log here is what makes the Calculator and Stats accurate — the two checkboxes below matter more than they look.

Adding a refill

Tap the + button and fill in the date (it can't be in the future), odometer reading, litres, and cost per litre or total cost. Fill in any two of litres, cost per litre and total and the app works out the third. If you then edit the calculated one, it recalculates one of the others instead, so the three always add up. Tap Cancel to back out without saving.

☑

Missed previous refill(s) — tick this if you topped up at some point without logging it. It stops that unlogged gap being counted in any efficiency figure, since the litres you did log wouldn't match the true distance covered.

☑

Not filled to brim (partial fill) — tick this if you stopped short of a full tank. A partial fill quietly skews two different figures in opposite directions, so this one entry is excluded from Max MPG / Min L per 100km, and the fill-up after it is excluded from Min MPG / Max L per 100km.

Why it matters

MPG only means anything when it's measured brim-to-brim. Fill up less than full, and that tank's litres understate what you actually burned — making that trip look better than it was, and making the next trip (which has to make up the difference) look worse. Tick the box and both distortions get excluded from your best/worst readings.

Fixing or deleting an entry

Every entry has a pencil icon — edit any field, including both checkboxes, at any time. Stats recalculate immediately, so if you logged a partial fill weeks ago without ticking the box, go back and tick it now.

The bin icon deletes an entry after a confirmation. Straight afterwards an Undo bar appears at the bottom of the screen — tap it to bring the entry back, and the list scrolls to show it.

Backing up and moving your log

Above the log are two labelled buttons, Import and Export, with an (i) icon explaining them. Both work with CSV files (a spreadsheet format you can also open in Excel or Google Sheets).

  • Export asks whether to save this vehicle or all vehicles. Files are named FDC <vehicle name>.csv or FDC all vehicles.csv, and a message confirms what was saved.
  • Import asks you to pick a file, then asks where it should go: the selected vehicle, another vehicle, or a new vehicle you name on the spot. Importing into an existing vehicle replaces its current log, and the app tells you how many entries that will replace before you confirm. A file containing several vehicles is imported into the vehicles with matching names, creating any that don't exist yet.

If a file can't be read, the app says so rather than failing silently — make sure it's one exported from Fuel Detour Calculator.

03

Stats

A running summary for whichever vehicle is selected, once you've logged at least two fill-ups. Use the vehicle dropdown at the top to switch between vehicles.

Efficiency

Average, best, and worst MPG or L/100km, in the unit chosen on the Settings tab.

Distance & cost

Total distance driven, your safe range estimate, and total & average spend.

Volume

Total, average, and largest fill-up — the largest one (partial fills included) doubles as a rough tank-size estimate elsewhere in the app.

Efficiency over time

A line chart with one point per fill-to-fill trip, oldest on the left, so you can see whether your economy is improving or slipping. Trips involving a partial fill or a missed fill-up are left out. Needs at least two usable trips.

Spend by month

Bars showing what you spent on fuel in each of your last six months with a logged fill-up.

Every row has an (i) icon that explains exactly how that figure is worked out.

What "safe range" actually means

It's the average of your three longest fill-to-fill distances on record — roughly what you've proven this vehicle can manage on a tank, based on your best runs.

Estimate, not a guarantee

Because it's built from your longest tanks, this number leans toward your best-case driving, not a conservative floor. Motorway cruising, stop-start town driving, a full boot, a cold engine, or bad weather can all shift your real range in either direction on any given trip.

Treat it as a rough guide, not a substitute for judgement — don't run down toward empty just because the number says you should still have miles left, especially before a detour or a trip somewhere remote.

04

Prices

Finds nearby fuel stations and sorts them by whichever single grade you're after.

Searching

Type a postcode, or leave it blank — or tap the location pin in the postcode box — to search from where you are now (you'll be asked to allow location access the first time). Set a search radius — in miles or kilometres, matching the unit chosen on the Settings tab — and pick a fuel grade: Unleaded, Super Unleaded, Diesel, or Premium Diesel. Choose whether to sort by Price or Distance. Whatever is selected — grade or sort order — is highlighted in solid purple.

The first search downloads the national price data, which can take around ten seconds — the app shows its progress as it goes. After that, repeat searches are near-instant.

Why four grades, not two

Unleaded and Super Unleaded run at genuinely different prices, as do standard and Premium Diesel. Picking one specific grade means the price you see and the order stations are sorted in are always the same thing — no mixing a station's cheap regular fuel with another's pricier premium.

Reading results

Each station shows its price on the left and its distance on the right (in miles or kilometres, following your Settings choice). Next to the price you'll see either a Cheapest badge — for the lowest-priced station or stations in the list — or how much dearer the station is, such as +2.1p.

Below that come the station's name — with its brand in brackets if that's different — and its area, town and full postcode, followed by how recently that price was updated ("Price updated 2 days ago") and today's usual opening hours, where the station publishes them ("Today: 06:00–22:00", or "Open 24 hours"). Sort by Price or Distance using the buttons under the fuel grades. The top bar shows when the prices were last downloaded.

Every station has two icons: a map pin to show it on the map, and a directions icon to navigate to it. Navigate hands the station's location to the navigation app of your choice — Google Maps, Waze, or whichever you have installed. Tapping anywhere else on a station's card also shows it on the map.

The map

Use the map/list icon at the top right to switch views. What you see depends on how you got there:

  • From a station's card or map pin — the map shows only that station, highlighted, along with your own location. Tap Show all stations to bring the rest back.
  • From the map icon at the top — the map shows every station, with a slider underneath the fuel grades. Slide it from the very cheapest all the way to all stations to declutter the map. The label tells you exactly what's showing, such as "Showing cheapest 7 of 133" — any stations tied on price are shown together, which is why the number isn't always a round one. It keeps its setting when you change fuel grade.

Tap any price flag on the map to select it and see a panel with its name, price, how recently that price was updated, today's opening hours and a Navigate button. Tap an empty part of the map to clear the selection.

How fresh is the data?

Prices are refreshed at most every 15 minutes, station details at most every hour, and an identical search within five minutes reuses the last result, so if you tap refresh when nothing has changed, the app tells you the prices are already up to date. Stations with prices older than two weeks, and stations marked as closed, are left out.

Price data comes from the UK Government's Fuel Finder service.

05

Settings

Your unit and appearance preferences, plus vehicle management. The selected option in each row is highlighted in solid purple.

Efficiency unit

Choose MPG (UK), MPG (US), or L/100km. This applies across the Calculator, Fuel Log, Stats and Prices tabs. Distances follow it: miles for the MPG options, kilometres for L/100km.

Appearance

Choose Light, Dark, or System to follow your phone's own setting. The app starts in Dark.

Vehicles

Add Vehicle creates a new vehicle — names must be unique, and you can also create one while importing a log. Below it is a list of all your vehicles; tap one to select it (shown with a tick). The Delete button at the bottom removes the selected vehicle and all of its fuel log entries, so export the log first if you want to keep it.
